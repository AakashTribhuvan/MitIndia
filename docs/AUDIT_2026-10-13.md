# MUDRA audit, 2026-10-13

An independent audit of the MUDRA ISL sign-to-text-to-speech project: what the system actually is,
whether its numbers are real, whether its approach fits the goal (dynamic, word-level ISL), and what
was faked, misleading or wrong. Every finding below cites the file or the command output it rests
on. Nothing in earlier documents was taken as correct without a check.

Scope: the whole repository at `a594c61` plus the uncommitted working tree of 2026-10-12 (the
in-progress "parked hands" / v8 live-webcam work), the local data in `data/metadata/` and the run
outputs in `experiments/`.

---

## 1. Verdict

1. **The recognition model is real and its reported numbers reproduce exactly.** Re-running the
   deployed v6 model on its test split gives 98.19% overall; 97.43% on INCLUDE pose clips, 99.00%
   on INCLUDE videos and 99.45% on team words. That is the same as `summary.json`, the model card and
   HANDOVER. No accuracy figure in the ML documentation is invented.
2. **Those numbers measure "a new take by a signer the model has already seen", not "a new person".**
   This is the most important finding:
   - 90% of INCLUDE test clips (735 of 816) have a training clip of the same word recorded 1-2
     camera files earlier or later.
   - Every team-word test clip (181 of 181) has a training take of the same word recorded within
     2 minutes (median 9 seconds).
   - The few-shot "new word" results have the same property.
   - The best evidence for new recording sessions is about **67%** top-1 (INCLUDE, 9 held-out
     sessions). No dataset in the project has signer IDs, so a true new-signer number does not
     exist yet.
3. **The training pipeline itself is clean.**
   - No normalisation statistic, temperature, threshold, checkpoint or stream setting was fitted on
     test data.
   - Augmentation and background samples are training-only.
   - The INCLUDE video and pose copies of each recording are always in the same split.
4. **The web app's home and marketing pages contained invented numbers and testimonials:**
   - fixed "96%/98%/94% confidence" recognitions of WATER, NAMASTE and HELP, three words the model
     does not know;
   - fake metrics such as "61% → 88% mastery" and "24ms";
   - three fabricated user testimonials with names and cities;
   - a false "zero biometric recording" claim and a false "WCAG 2.1 AAA" claim.

   These are now removed or corrected (Section 5).
5. **Live use on a real webcam is unproven.**
   - The only real-world evidence is the developer's stream log: 108 segments, 7 words added,
     correctness unknown. One full session recognised 0 of 11 signs.
   - Every "streaming" result in the documents comes from simulated streams stitched from test
     clips.
6. **The approach fits the goal** (Section 3): landmark sequences plus a temporal model, for
   isolated dynamic words, with a sign segmenter. The bottleneck is data from many signers, and an
   honest test of new people, not the architecture.

## 2. What the system actually is

### 2.1 Runtime path (verified in code)

| Stage | Where | What it does | Why this choice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Camera + landmarks | `app/frontend/src/services/handTracker.js` | MediaPipe Tasks in the browser: 2 hands × 21 points (every frame) + 33 pose points (every other frame) | No video leaves the device; landmarks are robust to background and clothing; runs in real time in a browser |
| Streaming | `services/signRecognizer.js` → `WS /stream` | One JSON message per frame (landmark coordinates only) | Low bandwidth; server-side model can be updated without touching the client |
| Hand assignment | `mudra_api/recordings.py:parse_frame`, `assign_hands` | Each hand goes to the signer's left/right slot by distance to the pose wrists | MediaPipe handedness assumes a mirrored image and is unreliable |
| Sign segmentation | `training/src/mudra_ml/streaming.py:SignSegmenter` | State machine idle → signing → ended from "hand raised or moving"; padding 0.3 s / 0.2 s; 6 s cap | The model classifies whole isolated signs, so the stream must be cut into signs |
| Classification | `streaming.py:ModelBundle` | 24 frames resampled from the segment → 162 features/frame → BiLSTM → 315 classes (314 words + `_BACKGROUND_`), temperature-scaled softmax | See 2.3 |
| Decision | `ModelBundle.decide` + `streaming.json` | background → no sign; p < 0.7 → uncertain; else ok; same word within 1 s → duplicate | Threshold chosen on validation streams |
| Client policy | `pages/CommunicatePage.jsx` | ok → added; uncertain/no_sign with top-1 ≥ 0.5 and hands visible → added as "unsure" | Fewer missed words, at a measured cost of −0.5 to +0.9 points of wrong words (Section 4, F11) |
| Sentence + speech | `services/islDictionary.js:buildMultilingualSentence`, Web Speech API | Fixed phrase table for known token sequences, otherwise readable words joined; en-IN / hi-IN / mr-IN voice | No NLP model; Hindi/Marathi exist only for phrases in the table |
| Custom words | `mudra_ml/fewshot.py`, Record page | Cosine similarity of the model's sequence embedding to recorded examples (prototypes) | Add words without retraining |

### 2.2 Features (`mudra_ml/preprocessing/normalization.py`, `schema.py`)

162 values per frame:
- **Hand shape (63 per hand):** each hand relative to its wrist, scaled by palm size.
- **Hand location (3 per hand):** relative to the shoulder centre and shoulder width.
- **Upper-body pose (27):** 9 points relative to the shoulder centre.
- **Presence masks (3).**

Before this, x (and z) are multiplied by the frame's width/height ratio so frames that are not
square do not distort shapes. Standardisation uses training-split statistics only, with one
standard deviation per block.

**Why:** shape and location are separated, so the same handshape at the forehead and at the chest
remain distinguishable. Normalising to the body makes the features independent of how far the
signer stands from the camera.

### 2.3 Model (`mudra_ml/models/sequence_model.py`)

| Stage | Layer |
|---|---|
| Per frame | MLP 162→256→128 |
| Over time | 2-layer BiLSTM (256 per direction) |
| Pooling | attention pooling |
| Head | linear layer to 315 classes |

2.6 M parameters.

**Why:** a small sequence model over landmarks is the standard approach for isolated sign
recognition (OpenHands, SL-GCN, HWGAT all use pose sequences). A Transformer was tried and tied (3
seeds), and velocity features were tried and were not better.

On the official INCLUDE split this model scores 97.4%, against published results of 85.6% (original
INCLUDE paper), 93.5% (SL-GCN) and 97.7% (HWGAT) on the same split.

### 2.4 Training (`mudra_ml/training/trainer.py`, `training/configs/experiments/mudra_words.yaml`)

- **Optimisation:** AdamW, label smoothing 0.1, 150 epochs with early stopping on validation
  macro-F1.
- **Augmentation:** small rotation, scale, shear and shift; landmark jitter; dropped hand
  detections; temporal crops. Mirroring is off, because handedness can matter.
- **Segment-style crops (50%):** a training clip is cut the way the live segmenter would cut it, so
  training samples look like what the app classifies.
- **Simulated close-up webcam (40%):** a virtual crop around head and shoulders, where low hands
  leave the frame.
- **Background class (25% of samples):** clip openings and endings, and synthetic face or hair
  touches, so the app can say "no sign yet".
- **Calibration:** temperature fitted on validation, bounded to [0.25, 5].

### 2.5 Data (`data/metadata/`, local only)

| Source | Clips used | Words | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| INCLUDE pose release | 4,247 (train+val+test) | 263 | official INCLUDE split; no signer IDs |
| INCLUDE original videos, re-extracted with the browser pipeline | 2,672 | 181 | each is the same recording as a pose-release clip; always in the same split as its twin |
| MUDRA team recordings (`C:\ISL\archive`) | 1,204 | 61 | 7 recording days; 41 words recorded on a single day; no signer IDs; 70/15/15 split by individual take |

**Why two copies of INCLUDE:** the pose release was extracted with a different pose model. The
re-extracted videos match the browser's landmarks, which closed a large domain gap (real-video clips
71.5% → 96.8%, v1 → v2).

### 2.6 Evaluation (`training/src/mudra_ml/evaluation/`, `training/scripts/`)

- **Clip level:** each test clip classified once.
- **Continuous streams (`eval_continuous.py`):** test clips stitched into 8-sign streams with
  synthetic transitions, idle stretches and fidgets. Framings: normal, close-up, close-up with
  resting hands, and desk (new, uncommitted). An optional frame-rate cap is available. Each output
  word is matched once to the true sign it overlaps most.
- **Unseen recording sessions (`combined_words_v2_session`):** 9 whole INCLUDE sessions held out.
  Sessions were found from runs of file numbers and first-frame clustering.
- **Few-shot (`eval_fewshot.py`):** model trained without 25 team words; prototypes from k
  recordings.

## 3. Is the approach right for dynamic, word-level ISL?

**Yes, with clear limits.** Evidence for and against:

**Fits the goal:**
- It models **movement over time**: 24 frames, BiLSTM, hand location relative to the body. It is
  not a static handshape or alphabet classifier.
- 263 of the 314 words come from INCLUDE, the standard word-level ISL benchmark. On that benchmark's
  split the model is at published state of the art (97.4% vs 97.7%).
- Isolated-word recognition plus segmentation matches the product: sign a word, pause, next word.
- Landmarks keep video on the device and make the model small enough for CPU inference.
- The alphabet image data was correctly not used: it is one signer, 100×100 px, and MediaPipe finds
  a hand in only 25% of the images.

**Limits:**
1. **New signers.** About 67% on unseen INCLUDE sessions, against 97-99% on the same-session split.
   Accuracy follows the number of training sessions per word: 2 sessions → 64%, 5 or more → 92%.
   This is a data limit (few signers per word), not a model limit: mirroring, stronger augmentation
   and pose dropout did not close the gap.
2. **No facial or mouth features.** ISL uses non-manual markers (facial expression, mouthing). The
   pose input has only nose and eye points, so signs that differ mainly by face cannot be separated.
3. **Isolated words only.** Sentences are lists of recognised words plus a fixed phrase table; there
   is no continuous or sentence-level recognition (a 101-sentence experiment reached 57% and was
   dropped).
4. **Vocabulary mismatch with the app.** The app's own headline words NAMASTE, WATER, HELP, YES,
   NO, GOODBYE, HOME and PERSON have no training data.
5. **Real-camera conditions.** The model was trained on studio-like videos. Low frame rates (6-8 fps
   seen live), close framing and hands resting in view are simulated, not measured.

**Conclusion:** the architecture is not the problem and should not be redesigned yet. The project
needs:
- recordings from many signers per word, with signer IDs (Record page; FDMSE-ISL, CISLR or iSign
  datasets);
- a test set of people who are in no training data;
- measured live use.

**Checked and found correct:**
- Normalisation statistics come from train only.
- Val and test datasets get no augmentation and no background samples.
- Early stopping and checkpoint choice use validation macro-F1.
- Temperature is fitted on validation; test ECE uses that temperature.
- The uncertain threshold is chosen on validation.
- The INCLUDE video and pose copies share a group and never cross splits (2,672 pairs).
- No group, raw-file or landmark-file overlap between splits.
- The unseen-session split really separates sessions (3 of 561 test clips have a ±2 neighbour in
  train, median gap 145).
- `/predict` returns the model's calibrated probability, with no constants.
- The per-word "got this right X of Y times" values come from real test predictions.
- The Progress page shows only locally stored real sessions.
- No `Math.random` is used to fake results.
- The live stream settings in `streaming.json` (committed version) were chosen on validation
  (`experiments/logs/cmp_val_v6_labelfix.log`, 523 validation INCLUDE signs).

## 6. Numbers that may be quoted (and how to say them)

| Claim | Number | Say it like this |
|---|---|---|
| INCLUDE benchmark, official split | 97.4% (pose release), 99.0% (videos) | "on INCLUDE's official split (signers seen in training)"; comparable to published results on that split |
| Team words, clip level | 99.4% (181 clips) | "new takes by the same people in the same sessions" |
| All test recordings once | 97.6% (973 of 997) | same caveat as above |
| New recording sessions (best proxy for new people) | 66.7% (videos) / 66.8% (pose), top-5 about 88-91% | "on 9 INCLUDE sessions never seen in training" |
| Team words, held-out recording day | 98.0% (99 clips, 20 words, combined v1, older model) | small; probably the same signer on another day |
| Simulated continuous streams (test clips) | 90-98% correct, 0-2.2% wrong (v6) | "simulated streams built from same-session test clips" |
| Few-shot new words | 70.1 / 79.5 / 81.6% for 1/3/5 examples | "same-session queries, hold-out model" |
| Real live use | not measured | the only log (developer, 108 segments) has no ground truth; one session recognised 0 of 11 |

Do not quote the pooled 98.2% without "1,495 rows, 498 recordings counted twice". Do not quote any
number as "accuracy for new users".

## 7. Still uncertain or open

1. **Accuracy for new people** is unknown. Next step: record 5+ people on the Record page (signer
   names are stored) and keep one or two people entirely out of training as the test set.
2. **Live webcam accuracy** is unknown. The v8 / parked-hands work (uncommitted) improves simulated
   desk-style streams (test: 38.8% → 74.1% correct at 7 fps). It is 2-4 points worse on test in the
   other framings and has never been tried on a real webcam. A decision on deploying it is pending
   (F25).
3. **The few-shot override of background decisions** (F14) has not been measured on idle movement.
4. **The sign descriptions in `islDictionary.js`** (used by Learn, Practice and the avatar) have not
   been checked against the ISLRTC dictionary; at least WATER looks like the ASL sign.
5. **The test split has been used for model choices** (F10). Final claims need a fresh, untouched
   test set, ideally of new signers.
6. **The team data licence** is unconfirmed; it must be confirmed before the repository or any model
   trained on it is made public.
